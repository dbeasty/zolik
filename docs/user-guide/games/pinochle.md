# Pinochle

The American bidding, meld and trick game, played with a doubled pack. Bid
for trumps, score your melds, then take counting cards in tricks.

- **Players:** 2–4, depending on the variation
- **Variations:** Partnership (4), Cutthroat (3), Two-handed (2), Double-deck
  (4)
- **Bots:** Easy, Medium, Hard
- **In-app rules:** the game's page › **Rules ›**

## The cards

A Pinochle pack has 48 cards: two each of the ace, ten, king, queen, jack and
nine of every suit. In each suit the cards rank **ace, ten, king, queen,
jack, nine**. Double-deck uses 80 cards: four each of ace to jack, with no
nines.

**Counting cards:** by default ("Counters"), aces, tens and kings are worth
10 each in the tricks you take. Queens, jacks and nines score nothing.
Winning the last trick is worth 10 more (20 in Double-deck). The tricks of a
hand hold 250 points in all (500 in Double-deck). The **Classic points**
option counts ace 11, ten 10, king 4, queen 3 and jack 2 instead, and a hand
is worth the same either way.

## The deal and bidding

- **Partnership:** 12 cards each, partners opposite.
- **Cutthroat:** 15 cards each, plus a 3-card widow. Each player plays alone,
  and the auction winner plays against the other two.
- **Double-deck:** 20 cards each, partners opposite.
- **Two-handed:** see below. There's no auction.

Starting at the dealer's left, players bid for the right to name trumps. The
lowest bid is 250 (500 in Double-deck). Each bid must beat the last in steps
of 10, up to 4000. Once you pass, you're out of the auction for this hand. If
everyone passes, the dealer must take the hand at the minimum (or, with the
option, the hand is thrown in). The winner names any suit as trumps.

In Cutthroat, the bidder shows the widow to everyone, adds it to their hand,
and buries 3 cards face down. Counting cards in the bury score for the bidder.

With **Passing cards** (Partnership only), the bidder's partner passes 3 or 4
cards to the bidder, who passes the same number back.

## Meld

Once trumps are named, everyone's meld is counted and shown on the table
during the first trick.

| Meld | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Run: A, 10, K, Q, J of trumps | 150 |
| Royal marriage: K and Q of trumps | 40 |
| Marriage: K and Q of another suit | 20 |
| Dix: a nine of trumps (not in Double-deck) | 10 each |
| Aces around (one ace of each suit) | 100 |
| Kings around | 80 |
| Queens around | 60 |
| Jacks around | 40 |
| Pinochle: Q♠ with J♦ | 40 |
| Double run | 1500 |
| Aces / kings / queens / jacks twice around | 1000 / 800 / 600 / 400 |
| Double pinochle | 300 |

In Double-deck there are also three and four times around (aces 1500/2000,
kings 1200/1600, queens 900/1200, jacks 600/800) and triple and quadruple
pinochle (600/900).

A double scores *instead of* the single meld: a double run is 1500, not
1500 plus 150. A card may count in more than one meld only if the melds are
of different kinds (sequences and marriages, arounds, pinochles). For
example, the queen of spades can count in a spade marriage, in queens around
and in a pinochle at once. A side's meld counts only if it takes at least one
trick (or, with the option, a counter).

## Playing the tricks

- The bidder leads to the first trick, and the winner of each trick leads the
  next. Play goes to the left.
- You must follow suit if you can, and you must beat the best card on the
  trick if you can, with a higher card of the suit led or by overtrumping.
- If you can't follow suit, you must play a trump if you have one.
- If two identical cards are played to one trick, the one played first wins.

## Scoring a hand

- If the bidding side's meld and tricks reach its bid, it scores them all. If
  not, it's **set**: the bid comes off its score.
- The other side (or, in Cutthroat, each other player) scores its meld and
  its tricks.
- Before leading to the first trick, the bidder may **throw the hand in**: the
  bid is lost, and the other side scores only its meld.
- The match is played to 1500 (1000 in Two-handed), counted at the end of each
  hand. If more than one side reaches the target in the same hand, the bidding
  side wins. If the top scores are level, another hand is played.

## Two-handed

Each player gets 12 cards. The next card is turned face up for trumps, and
the rest form the stock. The turned card is the last one drawn. If it's a
nine (the dix), the dealer scores 10 at once.

- The non-dealer leads first. While the stock lasts, you may play any card,
  and after each trick both players draw, the winner first.
- Only the winner of a trick may meld, one meld per trick won, and only while
  the stock lasts. Melded cards stay on the table and may still be played. A
  nine of trumps may be exchanged for the turned-up trump card, which scores
  10 and counts as that turn's meld.
- When the stock runs out, melded cards go back into their owners' hands, and
  the last tricks are played under the full rules above.
- Each player scores their meld as they lay it, plus the points in the tricks
  they take. The first to 1000 wins.

## Options

| Option | Choices | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Counting cards** | Counters / Classic points | Counters | How trick points are counted. A hand is worth the same either way. |
| **Minimum bid** | 200 / 250 / 300 / 400 / 500 / 600 | 250; Double-deck 500 | The lowest opening bid. Two-handed has no auction. |
| **Play to** | 1000 / 1500 / 2500 / 5000 | 1500; Two-handed 1000 | The score that wins the match. |
| **Passing cards** | No passing / Pass 3 / Pass 4 | No passing | Partnership tables only. |
| **Meld counts if** | It takes a trick / It takes a counter | It takes a trick | What a side must take in play before its meld scores. |
| **If everyone passes** | The dealer takes it at the minimum / Throw the hand in and deal again | The dealer takes it | What happens when nobody bids. |
| **Card points** | Hidden until the end / Shown as you play | Shown as you play | Show every side's meld and points taken as the hand is played. |

Also available: Opponents, Pause between rounds, When a player drops, Step
out, Hints.

## Tips

- Bid on meld plus a realistic share of the 250 trick points, not on hope.
- Partners: your meld counts towards the bid too, so help the bidder by
  passing your best trumps and aces when card passing is on.
