# Siete y Medio

"Seven and a half": the Spanish and Mexican cousin of twenty-one, played with
a forty-card Spanish pack. Get closer to 7½ than the bank without going over.
Played for chips, never real money. The app marks it "18+", and on phones
it's hidden from players under 18.

- **Players:** 2–8
- **Variations:** Mexicana, Cantina, Open table (Abierta)
- **Bots:** Easy, Medium, Hard
- **In-app rules:** the game's page › **Rules ›**

## The cards

Forty Spanish cards: the ace to the seven, plus the **sota**, **caballo** and
**rey**, in oros, copas, espadas and bastos. There are no eights, nines or
tens. The ace counts one, the numbers count their number, and the sota,
caballo and rey count half a point each.

## The table

Every seat sits down with 500 chips (by default), and the table minimum is 10
(by default). One player is the **banker**. The bank answers every stake out
of the banker's own chips, so no stake may be larger than the bank can cover.
The banker puts up no stake of their own.

## How a round works

1. **Stakes up.** One stake per round, and once it's up it can't be changed.
2. **One card each.** In Mexicana and Cantina, the first card is yours alone
   to see, and every card after it is dealt face up. At an Open table,
   every card is face up, the banker's included.
3. Every seat plays its hand in turn from the banker's left. **Ask** for as
   many cards as you like, or **stand**.
4. Over 7½ and your stake is lost at once, even if the bank goes over too.
   Exactly 7½ stands by itself.
5. **The banker** turns their own card over and plays last, against the hands
   still standing. A bank that goes over 7½ pays everybody still standing.

**Ties:** in Mexicana and at an Open table, a hand level with the bank loses,
because the bank takes ties. In Cantina, a level hand gets its stake back.

**Seven and a half:** beating the bank with exactly 7½ pays double the stake
(by default).

**Who holds the bank:** in Mexicana, whoever makes exactly 7½ takes the bank
for the next round. In Cantina and at an Open table, the bank passes to the
next seat every round.

## How the match ends

The table plays 12 rounds (by default), and whoever holds the most chips at
the end wins. A seat that can no longer cover the minimum sits out the rest
of the match. You may get up at any time and keep your chips. With cards in
front of you, the round is played out first. A seat that's away for longer
than **Leave if away for** is cashed out.

Also available: Pause between rounds, Opponents, Hints.

## Tips

- Half-point cards are common (12 of the 40), so a total of 5 or 5½ is
  riskier to stand on than it looks.
- With the bank taking ties, you need to beat the bank's total, not just
  match it.
