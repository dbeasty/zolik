# Šnaps

A two-player trick-taking race to 66, known elsewhere as Schnapsen. Win tricks,
announce marriages, and decide when to close the talon. Jokerless also offers
the 24-card version, **Šedesát šest** (Sixty-six).

- **Players:** 2
- **Variations:** Šnaps, Šedesát šest
- **Bots:** Easy, Medium, Hard
- **In-app rules:** the game's page › **Rules ›**

The app keeps the Czech card names: **eso** (ace), **král** (king),
**svršek** (Ober) and **spodek** (Unter).

## Objective

In each deal, be first to 66 card points. A deal is worth 1, 2 or 3 game
points, and the first to 7 game points (by default) wins the match.

## The cards

| Variation | Cards | Values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Šnaps | 20: eso, 10, král, svršek, spodek in four suits | eso 11, 10 10, král 4, svršek 3, spodek 2 (120 in all) |
| Šedesát šest | 24: as above plus the 9 | the same, and the 9 scores nothing (120 in all) |

## The deal

Each player gets 5 cards (6 in Šedesát šest). The next card is turned up for
trumps, and the rest of the talon is laid across it. The deal alternates. The
player who didn't deal leads to the first trick, and whoever takes a trick
leads to the next.

## Play

- **While the talon is open**, you may play any card. The higher card of the
  suit led wins, or the higher trump. After each trick, the winner draws the
  top card and the other player the next. The last card drawn is the
  turned-up trump.
- **Once the talon is closed or used up**, the strict rules apply: follow
  suit and beat the card led if you can; if you can't follow, play a trump;
  otherwise play anything.
- **Exchanging the trump:** on your lead while the talon is open, you may swap
  the spodek of trumps (in Šedesát šest, the 9 of trumps, and only once
  you've taken a trick) for the turned-up trump card.
- **Marriages:** lead the král or svršek while holding the other to announce
  a marriage: 20, or 40 in trumps. It counts once you've taken a trick. In
  Šedesát šest, no marriage can be announced once the talon is closed or
  used up.

## Ending a deal

- Reaching 66, on taking a trick or announcing a marriage, ends the deal at
  once.
- Going out scores **3** game points if the other player has taken no trick,
  **2** if they have under 33 points, and **1** otherwise.
- **Closing the talon:** on your lead while the talon is open, you may close
  it. Nobody draws any more, the strict rules apply at once, and you must
  reach 66.
  - In Šnaps, a closer who goes out is paid on the other player's tricks and
    count as they were when the talon was closed. If the other player goes
    out first, they score 2, or 3 if they had no trick then. A closer who
    runs out of cards short of 66 gives the other player 2 game points, or 3
    if they had no trick when the talon was closed.
  - In Šedesát šest, a closer who goes out is paid on the other player's
    count at the end. If the other player goes out first, they score 2.
- **Talon exhausted:** in Šnaps, if the talon runs out and nobody reaches 66,
  whoever takes the last trick wins the deal for 1. In Šedesát šest, the last
  trick is worth 10 more and the higher count wins the deal, scored as going
  out. At 65 each, nobody scores.

## Options

| Option | Choices | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Match to** | 3 / 7 / 11 | 7 | How many game points win the match. |
| **Card points** | Hidden until the end / Shown as you play | Shown as you play | Show both players' card points as the deal is played. Your own count is always shown to you. |

Also available: Opponents, Pause between rounds, When a player drops, Step
out, Hints.

## Tips

- Close the talon when you're confident of reaching 66 with what you hold. It
  stops your opponent drawing into a better hand.
- Keep track of points taken: knowing you're on 46 with a trump marriage in
  hand tells you to announce it.
